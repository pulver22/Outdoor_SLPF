Reviewer 4 of IROS 2026 submission 1263

Comments to the author
======================

The paper "Semantic Landmark Particle Filter for Robot
Localisation in Vineyards" presents a novel probabilistic
localization framework for vineyard environments. It uses
detected vine trunks and poles as semantic walls to resolve
corridor ambiguity in SLAM methods caused by repetitive
geometry. GNSS provides anchoring when visual observations
are sparse, with evaluation against RTK ground truth, noisy
RTK as GNSS anchor, AMCL, AMCL+NoisyGNSS, and RTAB-Map. The
method shows competitive or superior performance across key
metrics.

Major Contributions:
The approach employs YOLOv9 for instance segmentation of
poles and trunks, projecting them into a BEV fused with 2D
LiDAR and GNSS for particle filter pose estimation. While
innovative, the evaluation raises concerns given its
reliance on both GNSS and visual detection.

The authors test robustness to dropped detections but omit
the detection network's accuracy, hindering assessment of
landmark quality. Noisy RTK-derived GNSS is reasonable
experimentally, but canopy occlusion risks in vineyards
warrant analysis of severe degradation or GNSS denial
scenarios.

AMCL+NoisyGNSS provides a GNSS baseline, but no vision
method uses GNSS anchoring, obscuring whether semantics
truly outperform SOTA visual localization. To clarify this,
the paper should include a GNSS-augmented vision baseline,
or an ablation without the GNSS prior. Despite these gaps,
the authors deliver thorough metric evaluation and
ablations demonstrating robustness.

Technical Accuracy:
Primary concerns remain the missing GNSS-augmented vision
baseline and limited GNSS source justification.

Organization and Style:
The paper is well organized and logically structured.

Presentation:
The manuscript is clearly written and well presented.

Adequacy of Citations:
Related work covers the field adequately.
