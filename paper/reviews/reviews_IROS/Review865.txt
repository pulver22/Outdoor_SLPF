Reviewer 2 of IROS 2026 submission 1263

Comments to the author
======================

### Summary ### 
The paper "Semantic Landmark Particle Filter for Robot
Localisation in Vineyards" proposes a particle-filter based
localization framework specifically tackling the challenge
of robot localization in vineyards. It fuses 2D LiDAR, GNSS
and semantics from RGB-D cameras. Pole and Trunk detections
are integrated to address robustness of the proposed
Semantic Landmark Particle Filter (SLPF), especially with
respect to row correctness in vineyards. In these highly
repetitive environments, the authors claim that most
compared baselines will fail mostly due to the lacking
capability to correctly distinguish between adjacent rows.

### Evaluation ###
The problem shows an interesting example of real-world
robotic applications and some of the proposed ideas (using
semantic cues to improve localization robustness) seem
promising for the specific application. Also, I appreciate
the effort of the authors to address all comments made by
previous reviewers. However, I think that the newly added
experiments but also in general, the paper still lack
clarity in some aspects and raises some major questions
with respect to its methodology and especially also the
experimental evaluation which complicate an assessment of
the individual contributions.

With respect to the method my major questions are:

- What is the source of the odometry measurements? It is
never stated how the odometry increments are obtained which
can have a huge impact on the systems overall accuracy.
- The proposed SPFL framework is framed as fully
probabilistic but I am missing explanations on how the
uncertainties are actually modeled. Eqs. (6), (8) and (9)
introduce uncertainty weights without further mentioning
how these can be derived.
- I was wondering how the GNSS and the local odometry frame
are aligned. Does it require some form of initial heading
direction initialization / alignment?

Besides of these open points regarding the methodology, I
have some concerns with respect to the experimental
evaluation, especially the following points need some
careful revision in my opinion:

- Choice, Implementation and Usage of Baselines /
Competitions
    * What is the reasoning to chose as RTAB-Map as a
baseline other than that it is vision-based? It is not
mentioned in the Related Work / Introduction section, nor
is it stated why this specifically is a suitable baseline.
I find the comparison somewhat unsuitable  as RTAB-Map
seems to be to me a pure SLAM framework, not a localization
framework.
    * Also, are you sure that RTAB-Map was correctly
configured? Looking at Fig. 4 (column III), it seems to me
that also the turns estimated by RTAB-Map are always in the
wrong direction. This is why to me it looks like there
might be some misconfiguration with respect to the sensor
setup. I highly recommend to double-check the setup.
    * I would recommend to state which implementations of
the compared frameworks are used (for AMCL, I am guessing
the standard version which is part of the ROS Nav2
Toolbox). Furthermore, as (to the best of my knowledge) the
standard implementation of AMCL does not provide any
interface for AMCL+GNSS, I think it should be described how
the *AMCL+NoisyGNSS* baseline is implemented.
- Evaluation of GNSS and its impact on the accuracy.
    * Looking at Fig. 2, I am wondering if the RTK-GNSS is
sufficiently accurate to be used as a ground-truth for
quantitative evaluations. It seems to follow quite jerky
trajectories at some point or was that actually the
movement of the robot?
    * GNSS is called a "lightweight probabilistic prior"
but the results seem to indicate that without GNSS the
framework almost fails completely (Ablation Study Table
III).
    * Also, I find it surprising that the *AMCL+NoisyGNSS*
implementation does not benefit at all from the usage of
GNSS. Do the authors have any reasoning for that? (Might
also refer to the previous comment that details on the
implementation would maybe help to understand this
behavior).
- Usage of SE3-Alignment and Relative Pose Errors for
quantitative evaluation
    * I might disagree with some of the previous reviewers
but I do not think that the evaluation of relative pose
errors and SE3 alignment before APE are well suited for
localization frameworks. If so, it needs additional
information on the source of odometry measurements.
- Results of the Ablation Study: While in general, I
appreciate the effort to do ablation studies for the impact
of several modules of the system, I think the presented
ablation study raises a lot of questions.
    * The proposed SPFL seems to mostly benefit from the
usage of GNSS. The impact of the semantics seems to be
quite minor in comparison to that.
    * Why is the overall accuracy so much worse than AMCL?
Why is the GNSS then leading to a major improvement but not
for AMCL?
    * There is an ablation of removing pose smoothing
(`no_pose_smoothing`) but this smoothing of poses has never
been mentioned before in the method description.

Besides of the points above, there are some minor comments
to improve the clarity in presentation:

- Still some abbreviations are not introduced at all or not
at the first mention, e.g. AMCL (abstract).
- Introduce all quantities and provide reasoning for them
(e.g. MAD eq. (10), lambda_c eq.(12)).
- Explain non-standard metrics, e.g.: cross-track error
(XT).
- I would recommend to use consistent headers for Table I &
II as they are showing the same metrics.

Comments on the Author Response for a Previously Considered Paper
=================================================================

The authors provided an adequate summary of previous
reviews and showed significant efforts to address all
comments. A summary of changes has been presented. While
highly appreciated, I think that the paper needs further
improvement for publication.
