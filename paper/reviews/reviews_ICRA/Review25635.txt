Reviewer 1 of ICRA 2026 submission 3047

Comments to the author
======================

## General Overview

- The introduction underplays prior sensor-fusion
approaches (LiDAR + vision). Please expand the related work
to cover fusion methods that confront row aliasing and
appearance change, and state clearly how this paper differs
(design choices, inputs, assumptions, and capabilities).
The current motivation leans mostly on LiDAR failures in
repetitive scenes without showing how your fusion
formulation advances beyond earlier fusion pipelines.

- The contributions are not fully supported by the
literature framing. Map out which prior works addressed
similar challenges (semantic landmarks, BEV projection, GPS
priors) and which gaps remain for your method to fill.

- Justify the choice of a 2D LiDAR versus a 3D LiDAR. What
would change in your pipeline and in failure modes if a 3D
scanner were available? Discuss expected benefits and
limitations.

---

## Abstract

- Make explicit that the semantic information comes from a
camera (RGB-D) rather than from LiDAR alone; the abstract
currently describes stable object-level detections and
BEV fusion but does not name the camera explicitly.  

- Define AMCL on first use in the abstract (the expansion
only appears later in the paper).  

- Add quantitative results (e.g., APE/RPE improvements vs.
AMCL/RTAB-Map) to give readers a quick sense of effect
size. Values are only in the Results section.  

- State the LiDAR type used in experiments (you note 2D
LiDAR later; include that here so the link to
repetitive-row ambiguity is clear).  

---

## Introduction

- Specify the required navigation accuracy for the target
application (vineyard autonomy). Provide quantitative
thresholds (e.g., row-keeping tolerance in meters/degrees)
so readers can judge whether reported APE/RPE meets
operational needs.  

---

## Related Works

- Add works that fuse LiDAR and vision (and/or semantics)
specifically for agricultural rows or repetitive outdoor
structure; then state how your semantic walls + GPS
weighting differ in assumptions, inputs, or robustness. A
brief comparison table (sensors, priors, map type, runtime)
would help.  

---

## Methodology

- Implementation clarity: ensure the method can be
reproduced from the paper alone (data flow, parameters, and
all priors).

- Calibration/time sync: clarify if the camera and LiDAR
are extrinsically calibrated and whether streams are
time-synchronized (hardware or software). The paper
describes projecting RGB-D landmarks to LiDAR/BEV but does
not specify calibration/sync requirements.

- Map construction: you rely on a surveyed semantic
landmark map and then create semantic walls. Discuss
acquisition cost, maintenance, and robustness to
missing/shifted landmarks; provide guidance for
lower-accuracy mapping or partial maps.  

---

## Experimental Evaluation

- Study area: report the vineyard area and total path
length. You mention 10 rows but not size/distance, which
limits external validity.  

- Sensors: list the exact 2D LiDAR model, specs (angular
resolution, range, accuracy), and mounting; you already
list the RealSense D435i and RTK-GPS, but not the LiDAR
brand/specs.  

- Baselines: briefly explain RTAB-Map inputs and settings
so differences are interpretable; consider adding a modern
visual(-inertial) SLAM baseline such as ORB-SLAM3.  

- Qualitative clarity: several trajectories show noisy
estimates; help readers interpret by overlaying landmarks
and semantic walls on the trajectory plots and by adding
zoom-ins at row switches. Also, clearly mark where the
pseudo-structural walls appear in the figures.	

- Failure modes: the approach appears sensitive to
misclassified/missing landmarks (which you note in
Limitations). Please quantify the effect by degrading
trunk/pole AP or masking portions of the map and reporting
performance deltas.
